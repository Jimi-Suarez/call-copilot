# pclub discovery framework: distilled for the live coach
Jimi's own study notes on Chris Orlob's Advanced Discovery Masterclass (pclub.io / Caliber), reduced to the moves a live coach needs. Own words, not the course text. Lesson numbers (L#) let Jimi rewatch. Full notes live in the Project doc `pclub_discovery_knowledge_base.md`.

Scope: discovery only. The course does NOT cover pitching, demos, objection handling or closing. Those come from the Nutanix material, not from here.
Core belief (L2): sales is about the buyer's problem, not the product. Keep tags short and never invent product facts.

## Classify what the buyer just said (L16, L21)
- BUSINESS PROBLEM: a suffering metric the decision maker owns (close rate, cost, attrition, cash cycle). Example: "quote is up 30%".
- ROOT CAUSE: a symptom that leads to that metric (scattered data, no one has tested alternatives). No metric on its own.
- SOLUTION: she states a need that implies an action ("we need a comparison", "we need training"). A solution has no budget by itself.
- If she leads with a root cause or solution, treat it as the OUTER layer of the onion: peel back to the metric first, then return later to diagnose causes. The call is not linear (L21).

## Moves

### M1 Rules of engagement (L5). Early, within about 2 minutes
Trigger: call start, nothing agreed yet.
Do: objective (the outcome you both want), agenda of 1-3 items, and a decision ("decide whether a next step makes sense, or part ways"). Use soft language, then ask "Does that feel fair?".
Lines: "Do you mind if we talk about the agenda?" / "Here's what I'm thinking; tell me if you'd like a different agenda."
Callback at the close (L5): "At the start we said we'd decide whether a next step makes sense." This is how a dated next step gets booked.

### M2 Opening question matched to the buyer (L3, L8, L9, L13)
- Latent or cold buyer: "What challenges in [area] would be problematic if not solved in the next 6-12 months?" (L3, L13)
- Active or inbound buyer: ask what motivated them, stay there a while, then ask permission: "Do you mind if I go back in time?" then "What was the original challenge?" (L9). If she already volunteered the pain, skip this.
- Friction signals: irritation, "just show me the product". Change approach.

### M3 Peel the onion: the need behind the need (L14, L3). Most important move
Trigger: her first answer is a symptom or a solution.
Ask: "What challenge is going on in your business that's driving this to be a priority?" Also: "What's driving that?", "What's that leading to?". Optional: "What did that conversation sound like internally?"
Stop peeling when you reach a suffering metric the decision maker owns, with numbers. A sign you are deep enough: the power person starts answering.
Avoid: blunt repeated "why". Stopping at the first answer (usually too shallow).

### M4 Validate priority (L13, L15)
Trigger: energy feels low, or it sounds like a small fire. Mid-call is fine.
Ask: "Is this the challenge we should focus on, or are there things that would overpower it?" Also check who owns the budget and where this ranks on their list.
Protects against happy ears: she engaged only because it was a shiny object.

### M5 Summarise, then transition (L17, L23)
Use her words and numbers, then check: "Did I get that right, or am I missing anything?". Then move on with a question.
- After the problem: summarise the problem, then ask the open diagnostic (M6).
- Before impact: the summary includes the problem AND the agreed root causes (L23).
- Explain why you're asking sensitive questions: "I'm asking because your CFO will probably care about this." (L23)

### M6 Cause analysis: open first, then targeted (L18, L19)
- Open diagnostic (always first): "What's your opinion on why this is happening?" It lets her say her own cause and earns the right to ask targeted ones.
- Then about 2-3 targeted questions on root causes our approach addresses: "To what extent is [cause] an issue for you?" or context-led: "Many customers see [cause]; how is that showing up for you?"
- A lukewarm answer is information. Move to the next layer.

### M7 Quantify to a monthly cost of inaction (L24)
Trigger: the suffering metric is known.
Get: metric now, metric desired, why that target, and what the gap is worth. Express it MONTHLY (not quarterly or yearly: yearly lacks urgency). Napkin level is enough on call one. Ask for it in her numbers; never make up a figure.
Line: "Where is it now, where should it be, and what's the gap worth per month?"
If she gives a yearly figure, convert to monthly and check it with her.

### M8 Open impact, then targeted impact (L25, L26)
- Open: "What ripple effects is this having on the rest of the business?" / "What would get derailed if you didn't make progress?" / "Who else does this challenge impact, and how?"
- Softener: "Senior execs are only funding must-haves right now; where does this sit?"
- Targeted: 2-3 consequence questions that fit this persona, phrased "To what extent is [impact] showing up because of [problem]?", with an out ("is that a concern right now?"). Don't ask ten.

### M9 Future state and permission to pitch (L28, L30, L31)
Only after the problem and impact are understood.
- Open solution question: "What do you think you need to solve [the challenge we've discussed]?" It reveals her buying criteria. Outcomes: she lists what we do, part of it, or things we can't do.
- Permission: "Can I try a few additional ideas on you?"
- Targeted solution questions that call back HER own causes: "You told me [cause]; to what extent is it important that [capability]?" Use a question, not a feature statement.

### M10 Buying criteria (L29)
Know her list (formal or informal) of what she needs in a solution and why each item matters (it should map to a cause she named). Company attributes can be criteria too (staying power, stability).

### M11 Close with a callback (L5)
Trigger: she has agreed a next step is worth taking. Book a date, attendees and purpose. Do not accept "follow up next week".

## Calibration (L27, L23)
- Don't cram. Discovery unfolds over the first half of a sales cycle, not one call.
- Short or high-velocity cycle: a lighter version. One metric, one open impact question, "who else", and the open solution question.
- Doing even half of this is better than most reps.

## Phrase bank cues (paraphrased; adapt to your voice)
- Agenda: "Does that feel fair?"
- Peel: "What's driving that to be a priority?"
- Open cause: "What's your opinion on why this is happening?"
- Targeted: "To what extent is ___ an issue for you?"
- Metric: "What metric would improve the most if you solved this?"
- Impact: "What ripple effects are you seeing?"
- Permission: "Can I try a few additional ideas on you?"
- Summary: "Let me take a step back and summarise what I've heard... Did I get that right?"

## Quality tests (from the course)
- No-logo test (L4): could her problem description only be this account? Specific numbers, dates, named initiatives and people make it unique. Generic ("ramp faster") means shallow.
- Money follows pain that is a business problem, owned by the right person, and a top priority (L13).

## What the course does NOT contain
Objection handling, pitching, demos, negotiation, closing. A "who-based impact" lesson and the "underlying motivation" question are mentioned but have no lesson text. The nearest material is "Who else does this challenge impact, and how?" (L25).
